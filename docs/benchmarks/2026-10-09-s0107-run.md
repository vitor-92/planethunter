## `p_planet` and the severity rule

The model was trained on 338 known signals from sectors 69 **and 107**. The class separation below is therefore in-sample, not a validation.

| Class (sector 107) | Mean `p_planet` | Fraction with p ≥ 0.8 | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| `KNOWN_PLANET` | 0.86 | 76 % | 84 |
| `KNOWN_TOI` | 0.62 | 37 % | 104 |
| `KNOWN_FP` | 0.12 | 0.8 % | 132 |
| `NEW` | 0.38 | 20 % | 1339 |

**What went wrong with the rule.** `p_planet` was missing for this run, because `vet` ran before the model existed. It was computed afterwards from the stored test results, and the candidates were promoted again. The rule then in force let the ML promote a signal that had failed the deterministic vetting (without a veto) straight to P1. That produced **122 P1 alerts**:
- 119 of the 122 had failed the basic vetting.
- 115 of those 119 had failed the duration test.

**Cause.** The linear model has a coefficient of +0.30 on the log of T_obs/T_pred. Durations longer than predicted therefore *raise* p, when in fact they point to giant stars with a wrong radius or to systematics.

**Fix (`alerts/severity.py`).** The ML never decides alone in either direction. A signal that failed the basic vetting is rescued at most to P2. Result: P0 2, P1 3, P2 238.

**Model fix.** Make the duration feature two-sided, correct the prior and validate on sectors not used in training. This is PLAN §17 Tier 1 #2.
