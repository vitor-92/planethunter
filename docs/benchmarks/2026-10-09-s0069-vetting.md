## What was measured and fixed

| Problem | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Centroid by significance alone failed real planets | 11 of 41 confirmed planets with > 3σ; all with implied source ≤ 0.88 px | fails only if > 3σ **and** implied source (offset / depth) > 1 px |
| σ/√n error ignores red noise | 4 of 41 planets failed transit-to-transit consistency | out-of-transit block error (Pont+2006) in odd/even, secondary, consistency |
| TESS EB catalog contains hot Jupiters | the initial 9 "FPs" were WASP-18 b, WASP-100 b, HATS-16 b etc. | confirmed planet beats the EB catalog in crossmatch priority |
| Planetary occultation vetoed as an EB secondary | WASP-18 b (~340 ppm at phase 0.5) | veto only if the secondary is ≥ 10 % of the primary |
| Loose V-shape test | limit 0.45 caught no FP; 0.35 catches 8/18 FPs and flags 3/50 planets (grazing) | 0.35, soft failure |
| Detection gates as a soft penalty | signals with SNR 4.9 and with 1 transit became P2 | SDE, SNR and number of transits become vetoes |
| **Transits of different planets become a "new planet"** | HD 23472 (b + c → "10.29 d"), TOI-703 (.01 + .02 → "11.40 d"), GJ 143 (→ "8.80 d") reached P1 | transit-by-transit crossmatch: if ≥ 50 % of the transits coincide with known ephemerides, it is known |

Lessons measured in this slice:

- Noise floor of the narrow-window search: in 144 trials with random periods on stars without signal, median SNR 5.2, p90 19, p99 98. A fixed SNR limit does not work; the test uses the star's own p90.
- Multi-sector search with sectors years apart (span ~800 d) produced spurious aliases of 26–64 d; `period_max_days = 40` caps the grid.
- Hosts in the continuous viewing zone (south ecliptic pole) have 10–22 sectors: a new, deep signal seen in only one sector is almost always a systematic.
